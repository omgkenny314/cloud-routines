### Fundamentals corner

Episode 214 was titled **"Navigating Timeframes."** That is a real, verified title, and it happens to name the single most common way a new investor confuses themselves — so let me teach it.

A timeframe is just the window you judge a price move in. The same stock, on the same day, is falling on one timeframe and rising on another, and **both readings are correct.** Nvidia yesterday is the clean example: it rose about 3%, which on a one-day timeframe is a good day. But the chip index it belongs to had dropped roughly 11.6% over the three prior sessions. Zoom out to the week and that 3% is not a recovery — it is a bounce inside a decline.

The trap is judging a move on one timeframe while holding a position on a different one. If you bought Nvidia to hold for five years, yesterday's 3% is noise; reacting to it is paying a trading cost to change nothing. If you bought it Friday expecting a quick move, yesterday is real information. Same number, opposite meanings, and the thing that decides which applies is not on the chart — **it is your own holding period, which you set before you buy.**

So the discipline is to name your timeframe out loud first, then pick the chart that matches it. Long-term investor, weekly or monthly chart, and daily moves are weather you ignore. Short-term trade, daily or hourly chart, and you need an exit rule before you enter. The mistake that actually costs people money is drifting between the two: buying for five years, watching the hourly, panicking at lunchtime, and selling a thesis you never gave time to be right or wrong.

A basketball version, since it genuinely fits: a guard who misses four straight in the second quarter is having a terrible quarter and may still be having a career season. The coach who benches him over the quarter is reading the wrong timeframe. The one who benches him over a two-month slump is reading the right one. Nothing about the shots changed — only the window.

**Your assignment, which needs no transcript:** pick one thing you already own or are watching, write down the timeframe you are judging it on, and write down why. If you cannot finish that second sentence, you have found something worth more than this week's missing episode.
