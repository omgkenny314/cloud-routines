### Fundamentals corner

Episode 218 was titled "Leveraging Your Losses," and we are in the window where that stops being a mindset phrase and becomes a tax mechanic. So let me teach you the real thing, in plain language.

**A loss is not a loss until you sell.** If you own something that has fallen, you have an *unrealized* loss — it is real in your account balance but invisible to the tax code. The moment you sell, it becomes a *realized* loss, and realized losses are the ones that can do work for you.

**What the work is.** Realized losses offset realized gains, dollar for dollar. If you sold one position for a $4,000 gain this year and another for a $3,000 loss, you are taxed on $1,000 of net gain, not $4,000. If your losses exceed your gains, you can deduct up to **$3,000** of the excess against ordinary income — your regular taxable income — for the 2026 tax year, with anything beyond that carrying forward into future years indefinitely. That carryforward is the underrated part: a bad year can quietly lower your taxes for years afterward. Deliberately selling losers to capture this is what people mean by **tax-loss harvesting**.

**The trap that catches everybody.** The **wash-sale rule** says that if you buy a substantially identical security within 30 days *before* or 30 days *after* the sale, the loss is disallowed. That is a 61-day window with your sale in the middle, and the "before" half is the part people forget. It can also be tripped by things you are not thinking about: automatic dividend reinvestment buying shares back for you, or a purchase in a spouse's account. If you ever do harvest, turning off automatic reinvestment first is the practical move.

**On crypto, I have to give you a split answer.** Historically the wash-sale rule has not applied to cryptocurrency, and some 2026 sources still say that. Others say the PARITY Act changed it and the 30-day window now applies for tax year 2026. I could not verify the law's current status, and I am not going to pick a side on a tax question I cannot confirm. If this matters to your situation, it is a question for a tax professional, not for me and not for a YouTube show.

**Why the timing lesson is the real lesson.** Several sources make the point that late October beats December 31 for this work, and the reason is mechanical, not psychological: you need the trades settled and the 30-day windows clear, and December 31 leaves you no room. The general principle generalizes well beyond taxes — anything with a deadline and a waiting period has to be started before it feels urgent.

**One last framing, since the show's title invites it.** There is a difference between "leveraging a loss" as a tax action and as a mindset. The tax version is arithmetic and has rules. The mindset version — that losing taught you something worth the tuition — is only true if you actually changed the behavior that caused the loss. Both can be true. Only one of them shows up on a tax return.
