### Fundamentals corner

Episode 213 is about gaps, so let me teach you gaps — in words, without a chart.

**A gap is a hole in the price record.** A stock closes at $100 and the next morning the first trade happens at $110. No shares changed hands between $100 and $110. Nobody bought at $103, nobody sold at $107. That ten-dollar span is a gap, and it exists because the market was closed while the news that caused it came out. Think of it as a missing step in a staircase rather than a line on a picture.

**Why gaps happen.** Almost always because something arrived while nobody could trade: earnings after the close, an analyst upgrade before the open, or — exactly like this week — an announcement from a Chinese chipmaker that landed while the US market was shut. Buyers and sellers do all their negotiating in the overnight order books, and the market reopens already agreed on a new number.

**The idea behind "gap fill."** Traders talk about gaps "filling," meaning price later travels back across that empty span and trades at the levels it skipped. The reasoning is that a gap marks a zone where no real buying and selling ever established what the stock was worth — so there is no accumulated support or resistance there, and price can move back through it with unusual ease. A gap is a vacuum, and vacuums are easy to cross.

**And now the part you should be most skeptical about, because it is where this gets taught badly.** "Gaps always fill" is one of the most repeated claims in technical analysis and it is not a law. Plenty of gaps never fill, and the ones driven by a permanent change in the facts are the least likely to. The useful distinction is *why* the gap happened. If a stock gapped down because of a temporary scare, filling it just means the scare faded. If it gapped down because a competitor genuinely changed the industry's supply outlook for the next three years, there is nothing to fade, and waiting for a fill means waiting for something that may never come. **This week's memory selloff is live, unresolved evidence on exactly that question** — a gap caused by an announcement that might turn out to be real, or might founder on the commercialization hurdles. Nobody yet knows which, and that is precisely what makes it worth watching rather than worth acting on.

**The connected idea in that episode title: volume imbalance.** Volume is how many shares traded; an imbalance is when buyers and sellers are badly mismatched at a given price, which is what forces price to jump instead of stepping. Gaps and volume imbalances are two views of the same event — gaps are what you see in the price record, imbalance is what caused it. That is the link Trapper is drawing when he talks about "the data within the gaps": the hole is not the information. The reason for the hole is the information.

**The one question to carry into that episode.** Whenever you hear anyone describe a gap, ask: *what changed while the market was closed, and is that change permanent?* The answer decides whether the gap is an opportunity or just a fact about the past. That single question will keep you out of more trouble than any pattern you could memorize.
